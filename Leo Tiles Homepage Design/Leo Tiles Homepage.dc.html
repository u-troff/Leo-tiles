Our story

"Warm hearts make warm hands, make warm tiles."

Leo Tiles has been a family business on the edge of Cape Town since 1975 — eighteen people strong, still making tiles to order. Founder Pat Kelly's son Mark took over in 2012, moving his architectural practice onto the factory floor so design and production sit side by side.

Our clay is African earth: feldspar, iron oxide and quartz washed from Cape granite over thousands of years. It's hand mixed, pressed, shaped at the table, air-dried up to two weeks, then hand-packed into the kiln to fire into terracotta.

That slowness is the point. A tile forced out of the kiln too soon cracks, warps or fades early — ours don't, because every batch gets the full time it needs to dry evenly and fire through. It's why a floor we made in 1975 still looks the way it did on day one.

"You bought tiles from your Dad, Pat, way back — they're stunning, our house still looks amazing." — a returning client, ordering for a new addition

Our story

"We are Africans that make terracotta tiles from the African earth."

A small factory on the edge of Cape Town, 18 people strong, manufacturing to order since 1975. Pat Kelly's son Mark took over in 2012 and moved his architectural practice onto the factory floor, so design and production could sit side by side.

Our clay comes from Cape granite mountains — feldspar, iron oxide, alumina and quartz, washed into rich red soils over thousands of years, then hand mixed, pressed, shaped and fired into terracotta.

None of that happens quickly, and we wouldn't want it to. Each tile air-dries for up to two weeks before it ever reaches the kiln, then fires slowly over several days — time that gives a Leo tile its colour, density and character. It's why floors we made decades ago still hold up today.

"You bought tiles from your Dad, Pat, way back — they're stunning, our house still looks amazing." — a returning client, ordering for a new addition
